Legal

The agreements that govern using Tahlk.

Two different sets of documents apply here: the ones your practice signs with Greenbar to use Tahlk with patients, and the ones that govern this website itself. Both are listed below — this page is an index, not the documents themselves.

For your practice

What you sign to use Tahlk with patients.

Business Associate Agreement (BAA)

Between your practice and Greenbar Systems, covering Greenbar's handling of PHI as your Business Associate — including the managed proxy to Anthropic. See Compliance for how this fits together.

Signed during onboarding

End User License Agreement (EULA)

Governs your practice's use of the Tahlk software itself — separate from the BAA, which governs data handling.

Signed during onboarding

Patient Privacy Notice Template

A template Greenbar provides so your practice can tell your own patients, in plain language, how their information moves through Tahlk. Your practice adapts and issues it — it isn't a notice from Greenbar to your patients directly, and it doesn't replace your own counsel's review.

Provided on request

For this website

What governs tahlkscribe.com itself.

These are separate from the practice agreements above — they apply to visiting this site, not to using the Tahlk application or handling patient data.

Website Terms of Use

Governs use of this website — not the Tahlk application, and not patient data handling.

Website Privacy Policy

A note on this page

This page is an index of what exists and who it's between — it is deliberately not a substitute for reading the actual documents, and it isn't legal advice. The BAA, EULA, and Patient Privacy Notice Template are real documents handled through onboarding and your Greenbar representative; the website's own Terms of Use and Privacy Policy are drafted separately and published here once finalized. If your practice has specific requirements, raise them before you sign.

Need a document before you sign?

Ask for the BAA, EULA, or Privacy Notice Template directly — we'd rather you read them early than skim them at the end.
